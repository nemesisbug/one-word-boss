# One Word for the Boss

One word describing the boss. The whole entry, no caption, no explanation.

## The word

**Parrot**

Every office has met this boss: the one who repeats your idea back to you
in the meeting five minutes later, louder, as if it were theirs. "My boss
is a parrot" is a line a colleague repeats at lunch because it lands
instantly — no caption needed. It catches a real habit (parroting the last
person in power and taking the credit), it is not the first word anyone
reaches for, and it works for a boss in a kitchen, a warehouse or an office
without anything needing to be explained.

Why not the obvious words: "Micromanager" is the first word anyone reaches
for, so it loses on fresh; "Tyrant" is shock without a joke; "Bossy" is a
caption for a child, not a boss. "Parrot" is the one a colleague actually
says about the boss they report to — and says again at lunch.

## How it works

`main.py` scores a shortlist of candidate words on the judge's own
published criteria, in the judge's own order — funny on its own, rings
true, fresh, travels — and writes the highest-scoring word to
`/output/entry.txt`. The run is deterministic: same word every time.

```sh
python3 main.py        # writes "Parrot" to /output/entry.txt
```
